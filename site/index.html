    Before you swim

    Time since a discharge is not a swimming safety test. Pollution can remain after a release stops, and reports can be delayed. This filter is a display window, not a safe waiting period.

    Check official bathing water advice ↗
    What these reports can and cannot tell you

    These are overflow monitor reports. They show when a monitored overflow operates. They do not measure bacteria, discharge volume or how far it spreads. "Not discharging" does not mean the water is clean.

    Updates take time. Wessex says its map refreshes every 15 minutes and reports are normally available within an hour of a discharge starting. Missing, delayed, offline or inconsistent reports show as Unavailable, never as an all-clear.

    History covers about 24 hours. That is all Wessex publishes in its live event feed. Older dates come from each outfall’s latest event times.

    Bathing water ratings are yearly. The Environment Agency rates each designated bathing water Excellent, Good, Sufficient or Poor from the last four seasons of samples. It is not a reading for today. From 15 May to 30 September the EA also publishes a daily pollution risk forecast, which Coast Watch links to on Swimfo.

    Rain is context, not a verdict. Overflows usually follow heavy rain, but dry weather does not make the water safe and rain does not prove a discharge.
